Basic Course Information

Course Description:

Artificial intelligence is embedded in engineered systems all around us, from personalizing our movie and music experiences to assisting with tasks like autonomous parking and smart navigation. This course demystifies the building blocks and computational methods necessary to design and evaluate AI-driven systems using a scientific, data-driven approach. We will explore fundamental questions such as: How can unstructured smartphone data be transformed into meaningful features for predicting mental health? How does an email spam filter learn to differentiate between spam and important messages? How can AI models be systematically tested, refined, and validated?

This course introduces computational methods for AI systems, with an emphasis on applying the scientific method to AI system design. Students will learn how to formulate hypotheses, collect and preprocess data, conduct exploratory analysis, and iteratively refine AI models based on empirical evaluation. Topics include data visualization, feature engineering, and supervised and unsupervised learning. Students will gain hands-on experience in R and Python, use version control tools (Git/GitHub), and leverage generative AI responsibly as a coding assistant. Additionally, the course will cover handling missing data, working with unstructured text and spatial data, and evaluating model performance through systematic experimentation. By the end of the course, students will have the skills to design, implement, and critically assess AI systems through a structured, evidence-based approach, culminating in a project-based assessment that applies these principles to real-world challenges.

Learning Objectives:
  • Apply the scientific method to AI system design, including hypothesis formulation, data collection, model evaluation, and iterative refinement.
  • Develop proficiency in programming with R and Python, understanding when to use different tools and frameworks for AI-driven applications.
  • Utilize key libraries and packages in R (tidyverse, ggplot, dplyr) and Python (numpy, scikit-learn, pandas) to analyze and visualize data.
  • Curate, clean, manipulate, and engineer features from diverse data sources, including structured and unstructured data, for AI modeling.
  • Perform exploratory data analysis (EDA) to uncover patterns, detect missing data, and assess data quality before model development.
  • Design, build, and evaluate machine learning models, applying both supervised and unsupervised learning techniques to AI system development.
  • Assess model performance systematically, using rigorous testing, validation strategies, and empirical evaluation methods.
  • Debug, test, and refine AI systems, ensuring reliability and performance through systematic experimentation.
  • Leverage GitHub for software version control and collaborative AI system development.
  • Incorporate Generative AI tools responsibly to enhance the programming and AI development process.
  • Implement an end-to-end AI system, integrating data preprocessing, model training, and evaluation in a final project-based assessment.

In-Class Quizzes and Activities:

Short quizzes, programming exercises, and other activities will be completed periodically during class to reinforce course concepts, provide hands-on practice, and encourage active participation. These activities may include Poll Everywhere questions, short programming or debugging exercises, interpretation of code or results, and other applications of course material. Unless otherwise indicated, these activities are graded for completion rather than correctness.

Because these activities are designed to be completed during class, they generally cannot be made up. To accommodate occasional absences, your lowest in-class activity score before the midterm and your lowest in-class activity score after the midterm will be dropped. Poll Everywhere activities must be completed by logging into Poll Everywhere through Canvas to receive credit. Additional instructions will be provided at the beginning of the semester.

Labs:

Laboratory assignments (lab quizzes) will be posted on the course Canvas site (via the Quizzes feature) approximately every two weeks, typically on Fridays. You will generally have about two weeks to complete each lab. These assignments provide hands-on exercises in R and Python programming, along with conceptual questions that supplement class material and provide preparation for the course project.

Labs are open-resource: you may consult textbooks, online references, and generative AI tools, provided your use fully complies with the course policy on generative AI. This includes appropriately acknowledging, documenting, and crediting any use of generative AI on lab assignments.

While there is no time limit for these assignments, they are designed to take approximately one hour. Laboratory work provides important preparation for exams and practice applying computational and analytical skills. Important: the generative AI policy for labs differs from the policy for exams. While generative AI may be used for labs in accordance with the course policy, generative AI and online resources are not permitted on exams unless otherwise specified. For exams, you may use only the materials explicitly permitted for that exam, such as your own course notes and code examples provided in class.

Exams:

The two exams will be based on classroom material and discussions, assigned readings, and laboratory assignments. Each exam will include two sections: a closed-resource section consisting primarily of short-answer and conceptual questions, for which no outside materials are permitted; and an open-resource section focused on analytical problem-solving and programming. For the open-resource section, you may use your own course notes and code examples provided in class, but the use of generative AI tools and other online resources is strictly prohibited unless otherwise specified.

The Midterm Exam will focus on R programming and conceptual material from the first half of the course. The Final Exam will be comprehensive with respect to conceptual material from the entire course, while the programming and analytical portion will focus primarily on Python.

Course Project:

The course project will consist of a detailed data analysis using Python on a topic and dataset of your choosing. You are encouraged to select a topic related to your own interests, and the instructional team is happy to work with you to identify an appropriate data source and refine the scope of your project.

You will submit a brief project proposal describing your topic, research question(s), and proposed data source(s) by the date specified on Canvas. The final project should demonstrate competence in a meaningful subset of the computational and analytical methods covered in the course and communicate your analysis, results, and conclusions clearly.

Your final project must be organized as a reproducible GitHub repository containing the code, documentation, and other materials necessary for others to understand and reproduce your analysis. Final project deliverables are due on the last day of class. Additional requirements, evaluation criteria, and examples will be provided around the midpoint of the semester.

The Use of Generative AI:

Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, GitHub Copilot, and other similar technologies are increasingly part of modern computational practice. Learning how to use these tools effectively, critically, and responsibly is an important part of this course. When permitted, you are encouraged to use generative AI as a learning, co-design, and programming partner rather than simply as a tool for producing answers.

Appropriate uses of generative AI may include brainstorming and co-designing solutions, exploring alternative analytical or computational approaches, explaining concepts, generating examples, writing or modifying code, debugging and interpreting error messages, reviewing or critiquing code and analyses, and assisting with documentation. The specific expectations for generative AI use may vary by assignment and will be communicated when necessary.

You remain responsible for all work that you submit. You should understand the code, analyses, methodological choices, and conclusions in your work and be able to explain and defend them. AI-generated output can be incorrect, incomplete, biased, or inappropriate for the problem at hand. You are responsible for critically evaluating and verifying any AI-generated code, information, or recommendations before incorporating them into your work. Generative AI should support your learning and decision-making, not replace them.

You are also responsible for the accuracy and integrity of all references and sources in your work. Generative AI tools may fabricate citations, authors, titles, quotations, URLs, or other source information. You must personally verify that every source you cite exists, that the citation information is accurate, and that the source actually supports the claim for which it is cited. Do not cite a source solely because a generative AI tool provided or summarized it without consulting and verifying the original source.

As the author of submitted work, you retain responsibility for its accuracy, originality, attribution, and integrity regardless of whether generative AI was used in its development. The use of generative AI does not transfer responsibility for errors, fabricated information or references, plagiarism, unsupported claims, or other problems to the AI tool.

Co-design does not mean outsourcing the work. You may use generative AI to help develop ideas, code, and alternative approaches, but you are responsible for making the final design and analytical decisions. Submitting AI-generated work that you do not understand, cannot explain, or have not critically evaluated is inconsistent with the expectations of this course.

Meaningful use of generative AI on submitted work must be acknowledged and documented. When generative AI contributes to an assignment or project, briefly identify the tool used and describe how it contributed to your work (for example, brainstorming an approach, co-designing a solution, debugging code, generating an initial implementation, or reviewing an analysis). You do not need to document incidental uses that do not contribute to submitted work unless otherwise specified.

Generative AI is not permitted on exams. Exams are designed to assess your independent understanding of the programming, computational, and analytical concepts taught in the course. Generative AI use during in-class quizzes or activities may be permitted or prohibited depending on the learning objective of the activity; instructions will be provided when necessary.

Finally, do not upload confidential, private, proprietary, or otherwise restricted data or materials to generative AI systems. You are responsible for ensuring that your use of these tools complies with course policies, University policies, and applicable requirements regarding academic integrity, privacy, and data use.
